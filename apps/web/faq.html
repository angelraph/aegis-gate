FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

The basics
What is Aegis Gate?

Escrow for shielded Zcash. A buyer pays into a shielded address that the buyer, the seller and an arbiter control together. Any two of them can move the money; no one can alone.

Why does Zcash need this?

Zcash has no smart contracts, so there's no built-in escrow. Without one, private trades between strangers mean someone pays first and hopes. Aegis Gate uses FROST threshold signatures, which Zcash supports natively, to get escrow without a custodian.

Do I need an account?

No. Each person gets a private link for the deal. The link is your access, so keep it private and bookmark it.

Money and safety
Who holds my money during the deal?

The escrow address does. Moving funds out of it needs one signature that only two of the three key shares can produce together. The arbiter can't take the funds: every payout goes to an address the buyer or seller set.

Can Aegis Gate run off with the funds?

Not in a self-custody deal, which is the default. Your key share is created and kept in your browser; Aegis holds only the arbiter's share, and one share can't sign. The secret messages that create the keys are sealed end to end, so Aegis can't read them either. If you opt out to an engine-signed deal, the engine holds all three shares and you trust the operator; the deal page says which mode you're in. Details in the security model.

What if I clear my browser or switch phones?

Download your key backup from the deal page once your key is ready, and restore it on the new device. If it's lost, the deal can still finish: the other person and the arbiter hold two of the three keys.

What happens if the seller never ships?

Open a dispute from your buyer link. The arbiter reviews the deal and can approve a refund with you. Two matching approvals send the money back to your refund address.

What if the buyer never confirms delivery?

Open a dispute from your seller link. If the arbiter agrees you delivered, the arbiter and you approve the release together.

Is this real money?

You choose. Testnet deals use free test coins with no value. Mainnet beta deals use real ZEC, priced between 0.0002 and 0.00045 ZEC (about $0.26 to $0.60) so anyone can try it safely. The deal page always says which network it's on.

Why is there a minimum price?

Paying out of the escrow is itself a Zcash transaction with a network fee of about 0.0001 ZEC. A deal below 0.0002 ZEC couldn't cover it.

How do I know the engine isn't tricking my browser?

Before signing, your browser downloads the transaction itself and checks it on your device: it rebuilds the escrow from your own key, verifies every output against what will be on-chain, confirms the money goes only to the agreed address (plus change back to the escrow), checks the fee, and computes what it signs itself. If anything doesn't match, it refuses. Compare the security code with the other person too.

Privacy
What can people see on the blockchain?

That a shielded transaction happened, and its fee. Not the sender, the recipient, the amount or what was bought. Escrows live in Zcash's Ironwood shielded pool from funding to payout.

Who can see the details of my deal?

The three people in it. Each can see the escrow's activity through its viewing key, which can never spend. The deal's title, price and terms are stored by the escrow engine to show on your deal page.

Using it
Where do I get testnet ZEC?

The Valar testnet faucet gives 0.125 TAZ per day. Use a wallet that supports Zcash testnet and the Ironwood pool.

Why can't I release right after paying?

Zcash wallets wait about 10 blocks, roughly 12 minutes on testnet, before received funds can move. The deal page shows "confirming" until then, and the buttons work as soon as it's done.

Signing got stuck. What do I do?

If your browser shows a signing error, for example after the page was closed mid-signature, press Restart signing on the deal page. Nothing has been sent yet, so the deal goes back to waiting for approvals; both people approve again and sign fresh. Money never moves on a half-finished signature.

What address should I use?

A shielded unified address from your wallet. On testnet it starts with utest1 or zutest1. Transparent addresses aren't accepted, so your payout stays private.

Under the hood
What is FROST?

Flexible Round-Optimized Schnorr Threshold signatures. A group of people jointly creates one key, each keeping a share, and any threshold of them can sign together. Zcash supports it through ZIP 312, and the Zcash Foundation maintains the audited implementation Aegis Gate uses.

Has it worked on-chain?

Yes. On 5 October 2026 a 2-of-3 escrow was funded in block 4,465,795 and released by buyer + seller in block 4,465,954, on testnet after the NU7 upgrade. The transactions and the viewing key are on the proof page.

Is the code open source?

Yes, under MIT or Apache-2.0, at github.com/angelraph/aegis-gate.
